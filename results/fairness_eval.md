# Presentation-sensitivity test: native vs second-language phrasing

Same facts, two phrasings, scored by Jev (Problem Solving rubric, 1-5 scale; 0 = insufficient evidence).
Mean of 3 runs per version. *Gap* = native minus second-language score.

| Story | Raw native | Raw L2 | Raw gap | Restated native | Restated L2 | Restated gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Client about to churn | 4.77 | 4.60 | **+0.17** | 4.87 | 4.80 | **+0.07** |
| Late shipments | 4.80 | 4.60 | **+0.20** | 4.90 | 4.80 | **+0.10** |
| Stalled deal on price | 4.70 | 4.57 | **+0.13** | 4.87 | 4.83 | **+0.04** |
| Missed renewals | 4.90 | 4.70 | **+0.20** | 4.90 | 4.90 | **+0.00** |
| CONTROL (vague answer) | 2.30 | 2.30 | **+0.00** | 2.40 | 2.47 | **-0.07** |

**Mean absolute gap (4 stories):** raw transcript 0.18 → with restate step 0.05

**Inflation check:** restating changed native-speaker scores by +0.09 on average (should be near 0).
The vague control answer, as the app shows it (native / L2): Insufficient evidence (1/5) /
Insufficient evidence (1/5) raw, Insufficient evidence (1/5) /
Insufficient evidence (1/5) restated. Restating must not rescue it.

## The 2x2: knowing the job vs speaking well

Experts tell the same concrete story; bluffers give no specifics. "Struggles" answers are written as halting spoken
transcripts (filler, false starts, grammar slips). Score shown as the app shows it: a score out of 5 with evidence
coverage, or *Insufficient evidence* when fewer than 3 dimensions are evidenced. Raw transcript → with restate step.

| | Speaks well | Struggles on camera |
|---|---|---|
| **Knows the job** | **4.8** (5/5) → **4.8** (5/5) | **4.3** (5/5) → **4.6** (5/5) |
| **Doesn't know the job** | Insufficient evidence (1/5) → Insufficient evidence (1/5) | Insufficient evidence (2/5) → Insufficient evidence (2/5) |

**Polished bluffer with impressive numbers** ("40% uplift… $2M ARR", no diagnosis or options):
Insufficient evidence (1/5) → Insufficient evidence (2/5)

Limit: the scorer checks whether specifics were *stated*, not whether they are *true*. A fluent candidate who invents
a complete, consistent story would score like an expert; verifying claims needs references or work samples.

## Communication role-play (customer-facing roles only, scored on the candidate's own words)

| Reply | Score |
|---|---|
| Good reply, native phrasing | 4.70 |
| Same reply, second-language phrasing | 4.40 |
| Fluent but jargon-heavy and vague | 3.30 |
| Simple second-language English but clear | 3.87 |

**Fluency gap:** +0.30. **Construct check:** clear-but-simple minus
fluent-but-unclear = +0.57 (positive means the rubric rewards getting the
message across, not sounding fluent).

Caveats: 4 hand-written story pairs, not real candidates. Second-language phrasing was written by us, not by
second-language speakers. This tests the mechanism; it does not prove the system is unbiased.
